# Batch 3 contribution research

Completed 115 individually written contributions, reviewed 2026-09-26. Existing biography and identity data remain unchanged. Each entry was compared with its assigned expansion before choosing a new topic. Output: `lib/data/saint-contributions-3.json`.

## Method and limits

- Sources were retrieved through web open or search results during this review. Some sites returned a cache/internal error through open while the same page's substantive text was available through search; these are still retrieved source text, not inferred contents.
- Several Vatican State daily-saint URLs were inaccessible. Alternatives included Holy See documents, religious-order histories, diocesan sites, national museum/heritage records, and primary scriptural or patristic texts. No failed-only URL is knowingly used to support an addition.
- Examples of search-recovered pages: Mazzarello's FMA letter commentary; Ecuador's Assembly Act 99 (1946); Matthew's USCCB introduction; Mother Teresa's Nobel lecture (print version). Direct alternative sources were used for Maria of Gatchina (Saint Petersburg Metropolia), McGivney (his Philippine office), and Medard (City of Soissons).
- Early lives often lack reliable incident-level biography. Additions instead examine specific surviving inscriptions, manuscripts, monuments, literary witnesses, or later institutions. These do not authenticate the legends they depict.
- Older Catholic Encyclopedia articles were used critically. Outdated feast/status language, polemical characterizations, and obvious date typographical errors were not adopted. Specific examples include the John of Sahagun reception distinction, Marcellinus's spurious synod acts, Martial's later pictorial legend, and Maximin's undated cataloged sarcophagus.
- Miracle narratives are attributed to their devotional or hagiographical setting; medical or supernatural certainty is not asserted. Orthodox entries retain their ecclesial setting. Michael McGivney and Miguel Pro remain Blessed. Mary Mother of God is treated as Marian observance/title; Michael as an angelic scriptural figure.
- Reflections are original study/discussion exercises, not historical quotations. No source quotations were added. Each source URL is unique within this batch and each entry's prose is below its source's 200-word paraphrase allowance.
- SourcesChecked in progress is a conservative running count of distinct substantive pages inspected, including alternatives; it is not a count of all search hits or every failed open.
