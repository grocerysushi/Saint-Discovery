## Unresolved identities

- **nemesius:** The supplied biography describes the bishop and philosophical writer of Emesa, while December 19 identifies the Alexandrian martyr Nemesion in the French bishops' Nominis calendar. Searches for Nemesius/Emesa and Nemesion/December 19 establish distinct candidates. The cited Catholic Encyclopedia article supports the writer; Nominis supports the martyr. Nominis itself gives inconsistent descriptions of the martyr's execution, so no execution detail was adopted. Preserve unresolved status until the intended person is selected.
- **nicholas-of-toledo:** Searches for the exact name, Spanish priest, and September 10 primarily returned Nicholas of Tolentino (1245–1305, Italy), whose feast the Augustinians confirm. A Metropolitan Museum engraving is actually catalogued as Saint Nicholas of Toledo, but its catalogue does not establish the supplied twelfth-century Spanish priest. The site's existing unsupported text also appeared in search results; it was excluded as circular evidence. The engraving title alone cannot justify a biography or alias redirect. Preserve unresolved status.
- **nina-martyr-of-georgia:** Searches combining Nina, Georgia, martyr, 1908, 1938, and December 15 did not establish the supplied modern woman without a surname. GOARCH documents the ancient evangelizer Nina/Nino, called Christina in Western tradition and associated with December 15 in Western calendars. Searches also found other modern martyrs named Nina, including Nina Kuznetsova, but no evidence identifies the supplied entry with them. Do not infer nonexistence or substitute one of these people without evidence.

## Notable corrections

Michael McGivney and Miguel Pro remain Blessed in the consulted official and ecclesial sources. Marian titles are observances. Modern Orthodox saints are labeled Orthodox saints, with civil/Julian distinctions stated where applicable. Louise de Marillac uses May 9 in the current Vincentian calendar; Michael of the Saints uses June 8 in the current Trinitarian calendar. Maria of Gatchina uses April 17 civil/April 4 Julian. Mesrop's Armenian commemorations are movable, so no universal fixed date is invented. Methodius of the Slavs is distinct from the patriarch celebrated on June 14. Maximin of Aix is distinct from Maximin of Trier. Marcellinus the pope is distinct from the Roman official commemorated on April 6. Historical uncertainties and late traditions remain identified in the biographies.
