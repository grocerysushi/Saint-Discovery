# Biography expansion batch 2

Reviewed 2026-09-16. Coverage: all 115 assigned slugs from david-i-of-scotland through john-francis-regis.

## Method and limits

Each biography was individually rewritten from the already reviewed baseline and consulted historical/church sources. The first paragraph remains a concise identity summary. Subsequent paragraphs develop setting, concrete events, work, writings, legacy, and evidence limits. No fabricated quotations were added. This is AI-assisted source checking, not review or approval by a historian, theologian, or ecclesiastical authority.

Sources include Holy See catecheses and canonization materials, Vatican News and Vatican City State saint profiles, official religious communities (Jesuits, Salesians, Dominicans, Carmelites, Little Sisters), church/diocesan resources, Orthodox Church in America, French bishops' Nominis, and the historical Catholic Encyclopedia. The latter is an older reference with limitations, not a contemporary scholarly authority. Baseline source links were retained when relevant; not every secondary baseline link was newly fetched. At least one substantive reference was consulted per entry, with alternate references used when first links failed.

Most profiles deliberately remain 169–200 words where a single source supplies the evidence, respecting source summarization limits and avoiding invented detail. Eight fuller profiles have 300–322 words with multiple consulted sources: Edith Stein, Elizabeth Ann Seton, Francis de Sales, Francis of Assisi, Ignatius of Loyola, Jerome, Joan of Arc, and John Bosco. These are substantive expansions, not exhaustive academic biographies.

## Historical safeguards and source issues

Ancient papal biographies do not invent administrative reforms or martyrdom where early evidence is absent. Obscure early saints retain explicitly identified traditional narratives. Joachim's apocryphal sources are distinguished from the canonical Gospels; Dismas is treated from Luke's unnamed repentant criminal and subsequent naming tradition. Eulalia of Merida remains distinguished from Barcelona; Jadwiga from Hedwig of Silesia. Imelda Lambertini, Isidore Bakanja, and Franz Jagerstatter remain Blessed. Orthodox figures retain their ecclesial context.

Reported visions, Fatima experiences, stigmata, and relic phenomena are described within their religious tradition rather than as independently demonstrated historical or scientific facts. Holocaust and Indigenous mission histories retain the relevant persecution and colonial setting. John Berchmans is not described as ordained. John Climacus's disputed chronology stays qualified. Established reviewed corrections were retained where new source pages contained suspect dates (including John Cantius's canonization and Ezequiel Moreno's episcopate).

Failed/limited first-source access included Edith Stein's press-office link, Dominic Savio's first calendar page, Elizabeth the New Martyr's Greek Archdiocese page, Enda's original references, Frederick's individual Nominis entry, and John Bosco's Vatican State page. Working alternatives are included in the output. Francis de Sales's inaccessible secondary Vatican State link was replaced with Totum amoris est. Jeanne Jugan uses her congregation's biography rather than a shared canonization homily; Isaac Jogues uses an individual official Jesuit profile to avoid sharing the North American Martyrs page budget with Brebeuf.
