# Biography expansion batch 1

Reviewed September 16, 2026. AI-assisted editorial research; not ecclesiastical approval.

Coverage: 115/115 assigned entries. All retain their canonical slugs. Each has four to six paragraphs.
Before: 11,446 words; mean 99.5; range 70–122.
After: 22,726 words; mean 197.6; range 170–341.
Longer profiles (300+ words): 5: Anthony of Padua, Augustine of Hippo, Benedict, Catherine of Siena, Clare of Assisi.

## Research and limits

Existing citations were used as starting points. A bulk public-page retrieval returned readable HTML for 164 of 175 initial distinct URLs. Additional web retrievals and replacement sources were used when a page failed or was too thin. Retrieval alone is not treated as proof: relevant passages were inspected for the added facts. Existing short institutional pages and the public-domain Catholic Encyclopedia supply much of the first pass. Short single-source treatments remain under 200 words rather than stretching the evidence. Five fuller profiles use multiple distinct institutional pages.

Traditions and late miracle narratives are identified as such, especially for early martyrs. No reconstructed quotations were added. Date precision was reduced when sources disagree (notably Andrew Corsini). Existing saint/blessed distinctions and corrected identities were preserved. The calendars and group commemorations remain distinguishable from individual biographies.

Access limitations: original Franciscan Media Agnes, Benedict, Benedict the African, and Colette requests failed in the bulk fetch; the Irish Franciscans source supplies Agnes, Vatican News supplies Benedict, and browser retrieval supplied Benedict the African and Colette. The first Lourdes request timed out but web retrieval supplied it. The lay-Carmelite Brocard URL failed; the Carmelite Province of Venice page was read instead. Catherine Labouré uses the official chapel page because the Vincentian page failed. The broken USCCB All Souls link was replaced by Franciscan Media. The USCCB canon-law link and its existing calendar reference were not used to add new historical life events. OrthodoxWiki Aetherius was retrieved through web after a bulk-fetch failure. Carlo’s primary foundation biography supplements the canonization notice; the binary PDF was not treated as extracted narrative evidence.

Shared-source budgeting: Basil the Great adds the distinct July 4, 2007 papal audience; Basil the Elder retains the encyclopedia source. Ansgar/Blaise and Anselm/Apollonius share short calendar landing pages but have separate substantive sources. Cyril and Methodius uses Benedict XVI June 17, 2009, not Slavorum Apostoli used by another batch.
