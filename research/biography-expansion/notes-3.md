# Biography expansion batch 3

Reviewed: 2026-09-16. Coverage: all 115 assigned canonical entries, John Henry Newman through Nicholas of Myra.

## Method and limits

The previously reviewed identity, status, dates, and other metadata remain authoritative. Existing concise biographies were retained and expanded with specific historical context, institutional work, reception, and source limitations. All entries have at least four paragraphs. Eight fuller treatments (Newman, John of the Cross, John Paul II, Joseph, Mary Magdalene, Maximilian Kolbe, Teresa of Calcutta, Katharine Drexel) have six paragraphs and 339–367 words. Other treatments remain proportionate to the surviving evidence rather than inventing anecdotes to meet an arbitrary length. Each gains at least 90 words over the earlier reviewed biography.

Institutional pages were retrieved through web open/search, including Holy See biographies and catecheses, religious orders, dioceses, shrines, the Orthodox Church in America, and the Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum. Existing secondary historical reference sources were retained where useful for early saints and the history of a cult; they should not be mistaken for contemporary eyewitness accounts. No invented quotations, private thoughts, or new miracle claims were added. Orthodox saints remain explicitly Orthodox; popular legend and canonical Gospel evidence remain distinguished.

Citation lists include inherited reviewed sources as well as newly retrieved references. A listed inherited reference is not a claim that every URL could be freshly retrieved in this session. Direct source access occasionally failed or returned challenge pages; alternatives were sought and added as follows:
- Josemaria Escriva: working Opus Dei English biography replaced reliance on inaccessible regional path.
- Laurence O'Toole: Archdiocese of Dublin dedicated story supplied an alternative to an unavailable events path.
- Marguerite Bays: Dicastery for the Causes of Saints individual profile used instead of shared canonization booklet and blocked devotional site.
- Nektarios: dedicated OCA life added when GOARCH page was unavailable.
- Michael of the Saints: Trinitarian Order saints page retrieved; inherited calendar reference retained for commemoration.
- Louise de Marillac: congregation's English institutional history and French life/chronology retrieved through search.
- Matthias: Benedict XVI's 18 October 2006 audience supplied an alternative to a timed-out Vatican State page.
- Maria of Gatchina: Saint Petersburg Metropolia's canonization notice id=17969 retrieved; inherited detailed encyclopedia reference retained.
- Margaret Mary Alacoque: Vatican News historical Sacred Heart article supplied accessible additional evidence.
- Kizito: Munyonyo Shrine's account of the basilica and baptism retrieved after the seminary-hosted page failed.
- Margaret Clitherow: dedicated parish biography retrieved despite failed diocesan page.

Mariam Thresia uses the individual Holy See beatification biography for expansion; the inherited canonization booklet is relevant only to later recognition. Newman uses a dedicated Birmingham Oratory biography for additional depth. Kateri and Marianne use their individual sources, not the shared 2012 canonization homily used in another batch. Full source-page reproduction and verbatim quotation were avoided.

Historical restraint is especially important for early martyr narratives, Mary of Egypt, Martha and Lazarus in Provence, Maximinus, Maurus, and similar entries. Traditional associations are attributed as tradition and are not presented as established itineraries. John of Avila's and Jonas's old implementation-facing correction sentences were removed from displayed prose.
